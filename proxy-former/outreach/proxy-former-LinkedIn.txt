Same GPU, 35× longer context.

On a single 16 GB GPU, a standard Transformer runs out of memory at around 20,000 tokens. The architecture I've been building, ProxyFormer, trains on 716,800.

The idea is simple. Instead of making every token attend to every other token, each chunk of tokens elects a "proxy". Only the proxies exchange global information, and then they pass it back to the full-resolution tokens. Those tokens persist through every layer, so detail isn't lost for good.

What that means in practice:
→ ~21K tokens of history: 15.6 GB → 2.9 GB of GPU memory, and 12× faster training
→ Multi-needle retrieval (50 needles per document): 99–100% at 4× the training length, 92–95% at one million tokens
→ Language-modeling quality holds: WikiText-103 perplexity 21.01
→ Built from standard operators, with no custom GPU kernels

This is early, small-scale work by an independent researcher. It demonstrates feasibility; how far it goes at scale is still open.

The Proxy Token method is patent pending. It's free for academic and non-commercial research, and I'm open to commercial licensing and to partners with compute, funding, or real long-context use cases.
